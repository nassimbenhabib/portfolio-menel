Hydrogeology education and applications

Connecting geological formations, measurements and water chemistry.

During my Hydrology and Hydrogeology Master’s at Université Paris-Saclay, I learned to connect three types of information: the geometry of geological formations, water-level and discharge measurements, and water chemistry. Field investigations and modelling exercises helped me explore the following questions.

University coursework and practical exercises, with identified applications in research internships and field investigations.

01
Quantitative hydrogeology coursework and exercises

Which way does groundwater flow?

Using piezometric levels referred to a common datum, I learned to read hydraulic-head maps and estimate hydraulic gradients. Exercises on the Hallue aquifer then explored the additional information needed to move from a gradient to discharge or travel time.

A gradient describes a change in hydraulic head over distance. Estimating a flux also requires hydraulic conductivity; discussing transport velocity requires effective porosity and explicit assumptions about the medium.

02
Maisse university fieldwork and quantitative exercises

How does an aquifer respond to abstraction?

At Maisse, I participated in groundwater level measurements and pumping tests in three boreholes. Quantitative hydrogeology exercises taught me to read drawdown curves and discuss the influence of a hydraulic boundary on their interpretation.

Pumping rate, level datum and observation-point location give meaning to the curve. Interpretation then requires a time window consistent with the model assumptions and consideration of possible exchanges with neighbouring aquifer layers.

03
Geochemistry coursework and practical exercises

What can a water analysis tell us?

My PHREEQC training covered speciation, charge balance and saturation indices. Exercises on pyrite oxidation and goethite formation explored how iron chemistry changes as the oxygen input varies.

Calculated supersaturation indicates a thermodynamic possibility of precipitation. Its interpretation must be compared with observed mineralogy, environmental conditions and kinetics. These university exercises support the geochemical reasoning used during the research internship.

04
Application in the 2023 GEOPS internship

How can groundwater protection be assessed?

During my Beauce internship, I compared nitrate time series with borehole geology. Mapping the sites, identifying the aquifer layer sampled and examining changes over time provides a hydrogeological context for differences in concentration.

Nearby boreholes may sample different aquifer layers. Comparisons therefore need to consider sampled depth, continuity of the cover and genuinely comparable periods, together with the history of inputs and possible mixing.
